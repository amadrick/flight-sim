# Boeing 777-300ER (`b77w`) — modelling and flight-model data sheet

Body axes: origin at nominal CG, +X right wing, +Y up, −Z nose, metres. "d" = distance aft of the nose tip. Body z = d + noseZ (noseZ = -36.3). Values marked "~" are estimates; see Confidence notes.

## 1. Identity

- **Designation:** Boeing 777-300ER
- **Manufacturer:** Boeing
- **Role:** Long-haul wide-body airliner (twin-engine)
- **Operator / service:** Commercial
- **First flight:** 2003
- **Crew:** 2 pilots (+ cabin crew)

A very long, twin-engine wide-body with the largest turbofans ever fitted to an airliner: the GE90-115B nacelles are nearly the size of a 737 fuselage. The fuselage is a perfect circle (6.2 m) with a long, stretched cylindrical body, a relatively short, pointed nose and a sharply upswept tail cone; the wing has a 31.6° sweep and raked tips, and the main gear has six-wheel bogies.

**Must-get-right features:**
1. Giant GE90-115B engines (fan 3.25 m, nacelle diameter ~ 4.3 m) slung forward of the wing LE, mounted at y = ±10.9 m; tall pylons; scalloped chevron-free round nozzle.
2. Perfectly circular, long fuselage ~ 73.9 m; the upper cabin lines are straight; roughly 12 m of straight section aft of the wing.
3. Six-wheel main bogies (three axles per leg), the biggest of any twin; track ~ 11 m; two-wheel nose gear.
4. Raked wingtips (small ~ 0.6 m raked, 0.7 m extension); wing sweep 31.6° (quarter-chord) with a big inboard Yehudi chord.
5. Pointed, sloping nose with a sleek windshield (six cockpit windows), smooth fairing.
6. Upswept tail cone with an upward-hinged APU exhaust; tail fin 18.5 m above the ground; horizontal stabiliser ~ 21.3 m span.
7. Long fairings for the flap tracks, belly fairing (wing-body fairing) that blends into the wing roots.
8. Large cargo doors on the lower-lobe and overhead emergency exits above the wing.

Planform area from the table (both sides, root chord carried to the centreline unless noted): **437.6 m²** vs published reference **436.8 m²** (0.2 %).

- Root chord 11.5 m (fuselage side), kink chord 7.2 m (y = 12), tip chord 2.6 m; LE sweep ~ 35° inboard / 31.6° outboard, TE sweep ~ 10° inboard / ~ 20° outboard; dihedral ~ 6° (jig; root at body y ≈ −2.4, tip at y ≈ +0.8 on the ground); twist ~ −5° washout; thickness 11–12 % root → 8 % tip; supercritical high-aspect-ratio wing (AR 9.6).
- Low wing, root at the bottom of the circle; raked wingtip adds ~ 1.2 m.
- Slats: 7 per wing (LE slats y 4–32, slat chord ~ 13 %). TE: inboard flap y 3.8–12 and outboard flap y 13–23 (single-slotted, 25 % chord); flaperon y 23.5–27, aileron y 27.3–32 (~ 22 % chord); spoilers: 7 per wing (y 5.5–30), 12 % chord, used as speedbrakes in flight and lift dump on the ground.

## 5. Tail

- Horizontal stabiliser: span 21.3 m, root chord ~ 7.8 m (LE root d = 61.0), tip chord ~ 2.2 m, LE sweep ~ 36°, dihedral ~ 7°; elevator ~ 32 % chord; trimmable ±~ 10°.
- Vertical fin: tip at 18.5 m above ground (~ 12.6 m above the fuselage top), root chord ~ 11.5 m (LE d = 56.0, TE d = 67.5), tip chord ~ 3.8 m, LE sweep ~ 43°; rudder ~ 28 % chord, single section, ±~ 25°. No ventral fin; a tail skid under the aft fuselage.

## 6. Propulsion

- Two General Electric GE90-115B high-bypass turbofans (BPR ~ 9): 115,300 lbf (513 kN) each; fan diameter 3.25 m; the largest-fan engines in service.
- Nacelles: length ~ 7.3 m, diameter ~ 4.3 m, centre at y = ±10.9 m, ~ 2.0 m below the wing; intake lip at d ≈ 28.5 (about 5 m forward of the wing LE at that station), nozzle exit d ≈ 36.5; round fixed-area core nozzle plus an annular fan nozzle (exit radius ~ 1.0).
- Thrust reversers: translating cowl cascades on both engines.

- Wheelbase (nose to mean main, from table): **31.2 m**; track (outermost contact points): **11 m**.
- Nose gear carries ~9 % of weight with the CG at the origin.
- Gear positions measured from the nose tip: nose leg d = 7.9 m, main legs d = 39.1 m.
- cgHeight (body origin above ground when parked): **4.5 m**; all contact points at y = −cgHeight (level ground).

- Tricycle: nose gear with 2 wheels (r ~ 0.55 m), steerable ±70°, retracts forward; main gear: 2 six-wheel bogies (3 axles × 2 tires, r ~ 0.64 m) at y = ±5.5 m, retract inward/forward into the wing-fuselage bay; contact point (geometric centre of the bogie) given.
- Static attitude: level (deck angle ~ 0–1°); the bogie tilts ~ 11° on its axis when retracting.
- Gear doors: large belly doors; nose gear forward doors. No hook, no chute.

## 10. Performance

- Mmo 0.89; cruise Mach 0.84 at 11,000 m (TAS ~ 247 m/s); max at sea level ~ 170 m/s (330 KCAS Vmo).
- Service ceiling 13,137 m (43,100 ft); initial climb ~ 11 m/s at MTOW; time to FL350 ~ 25 min.
- Stall (landing, 250 t) ~ 64 m/s (~ 125 kt); approach ~ 72 m/s (~ 140 kt); rotate ~ 80 m/s (~ 155 kt at 350 t); take-off ~ 3,050 m at MTOW (SL ISA); landing ~ 1,800 m.
- Range ~ 13,650 km (7,370 nm) with ~ 396 pax. G +2.5 / −1.0. Roll rate ~ 5–6 °/s. Glide ~ 18:1.
- T/W ~ 0.37 at typical (2 × 513 kN); wing loading ~ 640 kg/m².

## 11. Flight controls and special systems

- Digital fly-by-wire with envelope protection (soft limits; "control wheel & column" pilots feel conventional): bank angle protection (increasing stiffness beyond 35°), stall and overspeed protection (the stick shaker warns, FBW limits pitch/AoA gently), auto speed protection with gentle pitch-up at overspeed, and tail-strike protection.
- Flap detents: UP, 1, 5, 15, 20, 25 (take-off up to 20, landing 25 or 30); slats extend with flaps ≥ 1; spoilers: speedbrake lever to flight detent, auto-speedbrakes on landing.
- Autothrottle, autoland CAT IIIB. Gear retract ~ 14 s. No hook/chute.
