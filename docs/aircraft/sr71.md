# SR-71A Blackbird (`sr71`) — modelling and flight-model data sheet

Body axes: origin at nominal CG, +X right wing, +Y up, −Z nose, metres. "d" = distance aft of the nose tip. Body z = d + noseZ (noseZ = -20). Values marked "~" are estimates; see Confidence notes.

## 1. Identity

- **Designation:** Lockheed SR-71A Blackbird
- **Manufacturer:** Lockheed (Skunk Works)
- **Role:** Strategic reconnaissance, Mach 3+ cruise
- **Operator / service:** U.S. Air Force (retired 1998; NASA flew two as research aircraft)
- **First flight:** 1964
- **Crew:** 2 (pilot + reconnaissance systems officer, tandem)

An extremely long, slender, matte-black delta-wing aircraft whose fuselage blends into sharp chines along its entire forward half. Two big engine nacelles sit at mid-semi-span on the wing, each ending in a long conical translating inlet spike at the front, with the two all-moving vertical fins canted inward, mounted on top of the nacelles. There is no horizontal tailplane; pitch and roll are by elevons along the trailing edge.

**Must-get-right features:**
1. Continuous chine line running from the sensor-nose to the wing leading edge; fuselage cross-section a flattened diamond/lenticular with sharp edges, not round.
2. Two engine nacelles at ~41 % of semi-span (y = ±3.7 m) with long conical translating inlet spikes protruding ~2–3 m ahead of the nacelle lips; nacelle ends with a long tapering exhaust/ejector section extending past the wing TE.
3. Twin all-moving vertical fins, canted ~15° inwards, mounted on the nacelles above the wing (the fins have a small tip chord and pointed tops).
4. Pure delta wing with ~60° LE sweep (outer), zero TE sweep; fine chord drop from root to tip; no horizontal tail; elevons: two per wing, inboard and outboard of the nacelles.
5. A long pointed nose cone ~ 6 m long (inch-per-foot-wise 12° half-angle), with a slightly upturned sensor-blister tip; tandem cockpits under separate framed windows (pilot window larger, RSO window behind).
6. Matte black overall with an iron-ball RAM paint; panels have corrugations on the wing skin (thermal expansion corrugations) running chordwise on the upper wing.
7. Tail cone: long, slender, ends in a point behind the wing TE; drag-chute canister at the tail.
8. Landing gear: nose single-strut and two main bogies with three small wheels each; the mains are in the nacelle/wing roots.

Planform area from the table (both sides, root chord carried to the centreline unless noted): **170.9 m²** vs published reference **167 m²** (2.4 %).

- Blended chine-delta wing; the chine inboard of y = 3.0 has ~72° LE sweep, the outer wing ~59°; tip chord ~2.5 m, root chord ~19.5 m at the centreline (extended), TE straight, 0° sweep (slightly forward at the root elevon).
- Dihedral ~0° (flat), no twist; thickness ~ 3.2 % (very thin double-wedge/lenticular airfoil), mid-set at body y ≈ −0.1.
- Control surfaces: elevons only; two per side — inner elevon y = 0.9–3.4 m? (not shown, inboard of the nacelle) and outer elevon y = 4.1–8.1; chord ~ 15–20 % of local chord at the wing; ±20° deflection. No flaps/slats/spoilers. 
- Pitch trim by elevon with a trailing-edge nose-up bias: no horizontal tail.
- Wing skin corrugated on the upper surface (outboard).

## 5. Tail

- No horizontal tail. Elevons provide pitch and roll.
- Two vertical fins: all-moving, canted ~15° inboard, root on top of the nacelle at y = ±3.7 m, root LE d ≈ 22.6, root chord ~6.4 m, tip chord ~ 1.6 m, LE sweep ~ 55°, slant height ~ 3.1 m (tip height 5.64 m above ground), area ~ 10 m² each; deflection ±~ 20°.
- Two small ventral fins/ 'keels': none; 2 small fixed ventral strakes under the chine at the tail (~0.8 m) are optional.

## 6. Propulsion

- Two Pratt & Whitney J58 (JT11D-20) afterburning turbo-ramjet-type engines: 25,000 lbf (111 kN) dry (military) and 32,500 lbf (144.6 kN) AB each (sea-level static); at Mach 3.2 the bypass/ejector flow provides ~ 80 % of thrust.
- Nacelle: ~ 17 m long (spike tip d ≈ 13.5, nozzle exit d ≈ 30.5), diameter ~ 1.7 m, nacelle centre at x = ±3.7, y ≈ −0.1 (just below the wing); the main inlet lip at d ≈ 16.5 is ~ 1.4 m diameter, spike tip ~ 3 m ahead.
- Inlet: axisymmetric mixed-compression inlet with a translating spike (fully forward below M1.6; retracting aft up to 1.65 m by M 3.2); spike angle ~ 12.5° cone. Bypass bleed doors on the nacelle sides open at transonic speed.
- Nozzle: variable convergent–divergent ejector; exit diameter ~ 1.35 m (nozzleRadius 0.68).

- Wheelbase (nose to mean main, from table): **12.2 m**; track (outermost contact points): **5 m**.
- Nose gear carries ~9.8 % of weight with the CG at the origin.
- Gear positions measured from the nose tip: nose leg d = 9 m, main legs d = 21.2 m.
- cgHeight (body origin above ground when parked): **2 m**; all contact points at y = −cgHeight (level ground).

- Tricycle; nose gear: single strut with two small wheels (r ~ 0.3 m), retracts rearward... (forward-retracting) into a bay below the cockpit (d ≈ 8–10); mains: two bogies, three wheels each (r ~ 0.47 m), retract forward and inward into the underside of the nacelles/fuselage bay at y ≈ ±2.5.
- Static attitude: the plane sits on its gear with the nose slightly down (~ 0–1°); take-off AoA limited ~ 10–12°.
- No tail hook. Drag chute: yes (ribbon, deployed at the tail cone) used on landing at ~ 150 kt.
- Tires: 3 per main leg; ~ 0.95 m dia; high-pressure nitrogen.

## 8. Cockpit

Pilot eye point (body axes): [0, 0.65, -13].

Tandem cockpit (pilot in front, RSO behind), separate framed canopies hinged at the side (rear hinge) with narrow, flat, heat-resistant windshields (fused quartz). Pilot eye ~ 7.0 m from the nose (z = -13 m), ~ 0.65 m above CG (nose slopes up from the keel). No HUD — conventional instruments and a vertical-tape engine/inlet panel.

## 9. Masses

- Empty ~ 30,600 kg (67,500 lb); max take-off 77,100 kg (170,000 lb); typical flying ~ 62,000 kg (after refuel); internal fuel ~ 36,100 kg (JP-7; 12,219 US gal, ~ 80,000 lb). Landing weight ~ 42,000 kg.

Estimated moments of inertia at typical mass (radius-of-gyration method, k = 0.12·span, 0.2·length, 0.22·length; ESTIMATE, derive properly if better data is available): Ixx(roll) ~ 256,000 kg·m², Iyy(pitch) ~ 2,658,000 kg·m², Izz(yaw) ~ 3,217,000 kg·m².

Derived at typical mass (62000 kg): wing loading **371 kg/m²**, thrust/weight at max thrust **0.48** (2 × 144.6 kN).

## 10. Performance

- Max speed: Mach 3.3 (design/limit; the usual sustained cruise is Mach 3.0–3.2) at ~ 24,000–25,000 m; max at sea level ~ 300 m/s (limited by dynamic pressure; ~ Mach 0.9; ~ 450 KEAS at low level is not used).
- Cruise: Mach 3.2 at ~ 24,000 m (TAS ~ 940 m/s ≈ 3,380 km/h; EAS ~ 185–200 m/s). Service ceiling ~ 25,900 m (85,000 ft; real ceiling classified > 25,900). Initial climb ~ 60 m/s (12,000 ft/min) up to ~ 4,800 m, then climb at M0.9 → M3 acceleration in ~ 20–30 min.
- Take-off: rotate ~ 100 m/s (~ 195 kt at ~ 70,000 kg, take-off ~ 210 kt), roll ~ 1,500 m. Landing: approach ~ 85 m/s (165 kt at 42,000 kg, AoA ~ 10°), touchdown ~ 80 m/s (~ 155 kt), landing roll ~ 1,100 m with chute; stall ~ 70 m/s (landing weight, clean).
- Range ~ 5,400 km unrefuelled (~ 3,000 nm); endurance ~ 4 h with air refuelling in flight to ~ 10 h.
- G limits: +3.0 / −? use +3.0 / −1.0; roll rate ~ 20–25 °/s at Mach 3, ~ 40 °/s subsonic; turn radius at M3.2 ~ 160 km (5° bank … autopilot).
- Thrust/weight ~ 0.47 at typical (AB), wing loading ~ 370 kg/m² at typical weight; glide ratio ~ 6:1 supersonic, ~ 9:1 subsonic (est.).

## 11. Flight controls and special systems

- Analogue stability augmentation (SAS/ pitch, roll, yaw dampers) and an autopilot; Mach trim and CG management through fuel transfer (fuel tank 'trim' shifts the CG aft at supersonic speed). No FBW protections.
- Inlet control: the automatic spike/forward bypass schedule (spike aft: ~ 0.66 m at M1.6 → 1.65 m at M3.2), engine inlet unstart: a violent yaw/roll upset (the sim can model as a ~ yaw 15–20° asymmetry for a few seconds). 
- Thermal limits: airframe skin ~ 300–316 °C at Mach 3.2; a Mach 3.3 limit due to compressor inlet temperature (CIT 427 °C) limit.
- Elevon travel ±20° with a 'pitch-trim lever'; rudders: all-moving fins ±~ 20°.
- Speedbrake: none; use throttle/decelerate in a straight line. Flaps: none. Drag chute for landing (and for tight runway use). Fuel: JP-7, tanks form the wing/fuselage structure (the airframe leaks on the ground).

## 12. Markings and finish

- Finish: ~#0b0d10 very dark blue-black "iron-ball" semi-gloss RAM paint all over (top and bottom). Highlights ~#1b1f26 on the surfaces facing light; the canopy frames black; the glass is dark, bluish tint (~#2d3a4a reflection).
- Inlet spikes and nacelle front: bare dark-steel/titanium ~#3c3f45; nozzles dark metal ~#2a2c30 with heated glow on afterburner.
- Markings: small, white/light-grey: "U.S. AIR FORCE" lettering is not present on the black airframe; small white USAF serial (e.g., fictional 61-7XXX) on each fin, ~25 cm characters; thin red "NO STEP" walk-area lines on the wing top near the root (tiny); red/white emergency-rescue arrows near the canopy; red fuel-nozzle arrows on the spine ("TOP FILL"), the ejection-seat triangle markers.
- Some surfaces have visible corrugation (wing top), and the nose cone is slightly tinted dark grey/green (~#232a2a) (the "chin"/'nose camera' windows are small dark glass).
