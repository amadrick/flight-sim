# C-17A Globemaster III (`c17`) — modelling and flight-model data sheet

Body axes: origin at nominal CG, +X right wing, +Y up, −Z nose, metres. "d" = distance aft of the nose tip. Body z = d + noseZ (noseZ = -25.5). Values marked "~" are estimates; see Confidence notes.

## 1. Identity

- **Designation:** McDonnell Douglas / Boeing C-17A Globemaster III
- **Manufacturer:** McDonnell Douglas (Boeing)
- **Role:** Strategic and tactical airlifter (STOL capable)
- **Operator / service:** U.S. Air Force (and allied air forces)
- **First flight:** 1991
- **Crew:** 3 (two pilots + loadmaster)

A big four-engine, high-wing, T-tail military transport with a deep, slab-sided fuselage ending in an upswept rear ramp. The 25°-swept supercritical wing carries four big high-bypass turbofans on forward-reaching pylons, and the wingtips end in tall, outward-canted winglets. The nose is blunt with a big greenhouse-type cockpit; the aircraft has external landing-gear sponsons on the lower fuselage sides.

**Must-get-right features:**
1. High-mounted wing, LE sweep 25°, big root chord (~10 m), tips with large winglets (~3.0 m tall, canted ~ 70° from the horizontal outward).
2. Four engines in pods slung forward of the wing LE on long pylons; the engines are low enough for the exhaust to blow across the trailing-edge flaps (externally blown flaps).
3. T-tail: tall vertical fin (leading-edge sweep ~ 40°) with a large horizontal stabiliser (span ~ 19.8 m) at the top; fin tip at 16.79 m above the ground.
4. Large cargo fuselage with a nearly constant box-like cross-section (rounded upper corners), a pronounced upswept tail and a lower rear cargo ramp; wide, flat belly.
5. Main gear in long, wide sponsons (landing-gear pods) along the lower sides of the fuselage; 6-wheel main bogies; nose gear forward under the cockpit.
6. Cockpit: big eight-pane flight-deck windows (4 large front + 4 side); nose radome rounded; crew door left front.
7. Para-troop doors on each side of the rear fuselage, aft of the wing; large rear ramp & door.
8. Engine nacelles: thin, long, pylon-mounted high-bypass cowls with a high-lip inlet; the thrust reversers are on the aft part of the nacelle (clamshell).

Planform area from the table (both sides, root chord carried to the centreline unless noted): **349 m²** vs published reference **353 m²** (-1.1 %).

- Root chord ~ 10 m (at the fuselage side), tip chord ~ 2.5 m, taper ~ 0.25; LE sweep 25°, quarter-chord sweep ~ 24°, TE ~ 8° back; dihedral ~ 3° (high wing, so low dihedral), thickness ~ 14 % root, ~ 10 % tip (supercritical). Twist ~ −3° at the tip.
- Vertical position: high wing, the root centreline at the top of the fuselage side: body y ≈ +2.2 (≈ 0.4 m below the top of the fuselage).
- Winglets: ~ 3 m tall, canted ~ 25° outward from vertical, root chord ~ 2.5, tip chord ~ 1.0; the winglet adds ~ 3 m to the effective tip height.
- High-lift: LE slats full span (~ 5 % to 98 % semi-span, 10 slats per side divided into 3 sections? — treat as 2 sections inboard/outboard of the outer engine), slats ~ 15° (take-off) to 25° (landing); TE: double-slotted flaps: inboard flap 14–36 % semi-span, outboard flap 40–68 % semi-span (the exhaust of the engines blows on the outboard-of-inner-engine flaps — "externally blown flaps"), flap chord ~ 28 %; flaps 0°/ 16° / 25° / 33° / 40° (landing ~ 40°, STOL: full 40° + EBF). Ailerons ~ 72–95 % semi-span, ~ 25 % chord; 
- Spoilers: 6 per wing (4 inboard, 2 outboard flight spoilers) on the flap shroud, ahead of the flaps, ~ 14–70 % semi-span; used for roll, for lift dumping and as speedbrakes.

## 5. Tail

- Vertical fin: height ~ 9.9 m above the fuselage top (tip 16.79 m above ground), root chord ~ 9.5 m, tip chord ~ 3.3 m, LE sweep ~ 40°, TE sweep ~ 12°; rudder ~ 30 % chord (split? two-segment, upper and lower), ±30°; fin root at d = 41.5 (LE) up to d = 51.8 (TE).
- Horizontal stabiliser (T-tail): span 19.8 m, root chord ~ 5.0 m, tip chord ~ 2.0 m, LE sweep ~ 30°, dihedral ~ 0°, mounted on the fin tip at 16 m above the ground; elevators ~ 32 % chord (full span); stabiliser trim ±~ 3° variable incidence.
- No ventral fin.

## 6. Propulsion

- Four Pratt & Whitney F117-PW-100 (military PW2040) high-bypass turbofans (BPR 5.9, fan diameter ~ 2.15 m): 40,440 lbf (~ 180 kN) each, 160,000 lbf (~ 720 kN) total.
- Positions: inboard engines at y = ±7.9 m? use ±8.0; outboard engines at y = ±16.5 m; nacelles slung ~ 3.5 m below the wing chord plane and forward of the wing LE: intake lip at d ≈ 17.5 (inner)/ 21.3 (outer, LE at y=16.5: d ≈ 28.3? — see below), pylons short; body-axes below.
- Nacelle length ~ 5.6 m, max diameter ~ 2.5 m; inlet lip radius ~ 1.0 m; the exhaust is a short, fixed plug nozzle with a ~ 1.0 m exit radius, pointing at the flaps (blown flap geometry).
- Thrust reversers: cascade/ clamshell on all four engines; they can be used in flight (safe at idle) to steepen approaches at ~ 15–20° glide path and for ground reversing (backup on the ramp).

- Wheelbase (nose to mean main, from table): **20 m**; track (outermost contact points): **10.2 m**.
- Nose gear carries ~10 % of weight with the CG at the origin.
- Gear positions measured from the nose tip: nose leg d = 7.5 m, main legs d = 27.5 m.
- cgHeight (body origin above ground when parked): **3.9 m**; all contact points at y = −cgHeight (level ground).

- Tricycle: nose gear twin-wheel, retracting forward (d ≈ 7.5); main gear: two 6-wheel bogies (three axles of two tires each), retract in the lower fuselage sponsons at y = ±5.1, d ≈ 27.5; wheel radius ~ 0.6 m; the bogie rotates 90° and retracts rearward-inward into the pods. Large, wide tires for soft field operation.
- Static attitude: slightly nose-down on level ground; the cargo floor is 1.7 m above the ground; "kneeling" is possible (not modelled).
- No hook; no drag chute; carbon brakes; tire pressure ~ 160 psi. Ground steering by nose gear ±60°.

## 10. Performance

- Max operating speed ~ Mach 0.77 (M 0.76 normal cruise) at 8,000–10,000 m; max at sea level ~ 180 m/s (350 KIAS, limited by q) .
- Cruise: Mach 0.74–0.77 at 9,000–11,000 m (TAS ~ 230 m/s); service ceiling 13,716 m (45,000 ft); initial climb ~ 12 m/s at MTOW (about 2,400 ft/min); time to FL350 ~ 30 min at MTOW.
- Stall (landing config, ~ 200 t) ~ 55 m/s (~ 107 kt); approach ~ 70 m/s (~ 135 kt, ~ 130 kt at 195 t); rotation ~ 78 m/s (~ 150 kt at 260 t; ~ 130 kt lighter); take-off roll ~ 2,300 m at MTOW (~ 1,100 m at 200 t); STOL landing roll ~ 900 m (3,000 ft) with thrust reversers; steep approach up to ~ 6°.
- Range ~ 4,480 km with 72 t payload (2,420 nm); ferry range ~ 8,700 km (4,700 nm).
- G limits: +2.5 / −1.0 (flaps down +2.0). Roll rate ~ 12 °/s. Glide ratio ~ 15:1.
- T/W ~ 0.37 at typical (4 × 180 kN / 200 t = 0.37) and ~ 0.28 at MTOW; wing loading ~ 570 kg/m² at typical.

## 11. Flight controls and special systems

- Quadruple-redundant fly-by-wire (the C-17 was the first transport with full digital FBW). Control laws: pitch (C*) with envelope protection (AoA ~ 17° limit, stall protection with stick pusher), roll rate command, yaw damper; automatic EBF (externally blown flaps) with the flaps at 40° + a nose-up pitching moment compensation; autothrottle, "heads-up" speed/AoA indications.
- Flaps: 0 (up), 16° (take-off), 25° (approach), 33° (landing), 40° (STOL landing); slats: 15° / 25°; spoilers ground lift-dump on touchdown, and in-flight speedbrake (up to ~ 45° symmetric).
- Thrust reversers on all engines for ground use and for in-flight steep descent (must be at idle). No hook, no drag chute. Wheel brakes: carbon. Max sink at touchdown ~ 3 m/s (design).

## 12. Markings and finish

- Finish: USAF "Compass Ghost" grey overall ~ #8a9096 (FS 36118/36270 light mix; some units in slightly darker "European One" green (~ #3f5233 / #566648 / #223020), now rare). Under-surfaces same grey; the radome dark grey ~ #55595e; the winglets same grey.
- National insignia: a low-visibility roundel and bar (~ #4a4f54) on the fuselage (aft of the cockpit, left & right); a flag on the fin (small); tail code (two letters) and the squadron fictional crest on the fin; "U.S. AIR FORCE" in black block letters (~ #1c1e20) on the forward fuselage sides, above the windows; aircraft serial in black on the fin.
- Walkway: thin black/yellow lines on the wing root; rescue arrows (red) at the paratroop doors; red "DANGER – INTAKE/ EXHAUST" boxes on the nacelles; the cargo ramp area has black anti-slip.
- Windows: the eight cockpit panes are tinted dark; the pilot/cargo door outlines are thin.
